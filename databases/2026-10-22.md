官方原文证据（保留基准及适用条件）：
3. The threshold: an unseen group's partial values are bounded by the current heap tops, so its merged value is bounded by their combination — the sum of the heads for the subadditive bound, the best head for the extremum bounds. As soon as that bound cannot strictly beat the worst kept candidate, no unseen group can enter the result and the merge stops without touching the remaining groups.

The result is exactly the same as without the optimization — the bounds are rigorous, so this is not an approximation.

The existing \`query\_plan\_aggregation\_bucket\_top\_k\` setting controls both conversion-stage selection and threshold merging. The planner and aggregator choose the algorithm automatically according to the aggregate, query shape, and distribution of partial states. Setting it to \`0\` disables both paths; \`query\_plan\_enable\_optimizations = 0\` also disables both.

Scope, driven by measurements on 40M-row / ~9M-group datasets:
- The extremum bounds of \`min\`/\`max\` engage for any number of per-thread tables: they converge right after the top n candidates are found. On a heavy-hitter distribution they merged 2560 groups instead of ~9M and left ~9.6M cells untouched.
